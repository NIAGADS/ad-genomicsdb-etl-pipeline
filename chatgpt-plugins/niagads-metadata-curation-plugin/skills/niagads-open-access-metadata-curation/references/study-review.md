# Source collection and study review

## Collect the materials

Ask for the accession and file(s) if unknown. A DOI, PMID, PMCID, manuscript title, repository link, submitter README, data dictionary, file manifest or existing metadata can be a starting point. Accept partial inputs; retrieve what is accessible before asking for duplicates. Support multiple files per accession and targeted review of existing JSON.

Discover manuscripts through PubMed, PMC, Europe PMC, publisher sites or open-access repositories. Follow links to associated supplementary methods, tables, data notes and publicly available submission materials, including NIAGADS dataset pages. Check that identifiers resolve to the intended publication/version. Retrieve actual contents of supplements where possible, including spreadsheets and PDF tables; a link or abstract does not establish full-text access. Record source URLs, document versions, section/table/sheet/page references and access gaps. Do not bypass paywalls or permissions; request uploads or authorized access. Do not ask for raw individual-level participant data. File names, manifests and aggregate results are normally sufficient.

Distinguish manuscript-reported analysis details from submitter file-specific specifications and processing information. Publication-wide results are not necessarily the submitted dataset. Flag missing or conflicting evidence without assuming a universal source precedence.

## Study overview (separate checkpoint)

Provide a brief, scientifically focused overview: research objectives and traits; populations/cohorts; major experimental/statistical methods; distinct types of analyses; and a concise inventory of supplementary materials with what each contains. Identify relevant locations and unavailable materials. Do not produce a full literature review or draft JSON. Invite questions about the paper and supplements, and explicitly pause before experimental design reconciliation. The curator can ask successive questions without automatically advancing.
